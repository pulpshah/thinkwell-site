What engineering changes

  • DNS. thinkwell.work and *.thinkwell.work both point at the app. On Vercel, a wildcard needs Vercel's nameservers so Vercel can issue the wildcard certificate. Source: Vercel docs, domain management
  • One deployment for every customer. Middleware reads the first label of the host (monument), finds that Organization and scopes the request to it. An unknown name forwards to thinkwell.work.
  • Names. The Organization gains a slug: lowercase letters, numbers and single hyphens, unique, and never a reserved word such as www, app, api, auth, admin, mail, relay, status, help or docs.
  • One sign-in. Google and Microsoft return to one callback on thinkwell.work, and the thinkwell_pulp_session cookie is set for .thinkwell.work so every subdomain reads it.
  • Who may sign in. Today an email allowlist decides (lib/auth/access-control.ts). Once customers sign up on thinkwell.cloud, a Seat in an Organization decides (ADR-0064), and Pulp staff keep their own door.

What engineering changes

  • DNS. thinkwell.blog and *.thinkwell.blog point at the blog app: today's pulp101, which already renders pieces, cues, discussions and scores.
  • One deployment for every site. The host names a Site, and every read is filtered to it: pieces, discussions, contributions, votes and reception events. pulp101 serves one site today.
  • Publishing stays one way. Thinkwell writes and the blog reads. Deliver already writes pieces into the blog's database; it gains the Site.
  • Participants are not Seats. They join free with an email or a phone number, one account for every site. Any extra check a site asks for comes from the Engagement Package: email, code, text, passkey or government ID.
  • Reception per site. Reader events are captured on the blog and read by Thinkwell (ADR-0039), now filtered by Site.
  • A separate domain on purpose. Pages full of participant posts never share a cookie domain with the app on .work.
  • Your own domain, later. dep.domain is marked coming soon: a customer's own address will point at its site.

Under the hood sign-up

What engineering builds

  • Sign-up runs on the app, at the .cloud name. thinkwell.cloud/start and /contact are served by the Thinkwell app's deployment, routed by host, so sign-up writes straight to the app's database. The marketing pages around them can stay static.
  • One handoff code, two new uses. The sign-in cookie covers every name under thinkwell.work, but it can't cross from .cloud to .work, or from .work to .blog. For those two moves the app mints a one-time code (60 seconds, single use, bound to the target name) and the target trades it for its own session. It is the app's mobile handoff pattern (app/api/auth/mobile-handoff), pointed at a name instead of the phone app.
  • Sign-up path. lib/entitlements/signUp.ts makes a Lite Organization of one for every uninvited person today. A person who arrives from /start/pro gets no Lite Organization; step 4 makes the Pro one, held as pending until Stripe says paid.
  • Stripe billing is new. Checkout in subscription mode, one price per seat, the Organization's id in metadata, and a webhook that turns the plan on. Stripe already runs Real ID checks in both apps.
  • Enterprise needs no new money path. Staff open the pilot with the staff editor that exists; the order is signed through the e-signature flow the app already has, then invoiced.

  • The Site record (pulp101): Organization, name, theme (accent, face: the kit's CampaignSiteTheme), homepage words, featured piece, status (made, live, paused) and version. Publishing a new version follows the workspace's approvals and writes an audit event.
  • Dye's site layer. The theme record holds choices, never colors. Dye maps the choice to tokens (--site-accent, --site-accent-text, --site-on-accent, --site-face) for each mode and the blog serves them in one style block. No raw color reaches a part.
  • Checked ahead of time. Five accents times two faces is ten looks; times three modes is thirty. CI checks all thirty for contrast, so a customer can't pick a look that fails.
  • Later, not now. A customer's own colors, logo colors and own domain (coming soon in the catalog) go through the same layer once Shah opens them.
